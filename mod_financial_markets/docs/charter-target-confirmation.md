# Charter target and confirmation

Opening the charter records the actual coin, its permanent ID, current controller, issuer and maker accounts, and the viewer's **primary private account**. The event displays those captured IDs and financial figures. Selecting another coin or active wallet afterward does not redirect its actions. Project receipts and promotion fees continue to use the primary account, as their existing production effects require.

Each opening advances the actor's charter nonce and keeps its own event scope. A newer charter invalidates older action tickets. Closing an older window cannot invalidate the newer one. An accepted ticket is consumed before invoking its business effect, so another confirmation cannot repeat the operation. A failed business attempt requires a newly opened charter; the nonce never substitutes for authorization.

Every option checks the captured live object again: coin identity and lifecycle, current controller and bound-entity authority, private primary account identity, holder and freeze status, and unchanged issuer/maker counterparties. Permanently renouncing issuance additionally requires the supply and cap reviewed in that charter to remain unchanged. Offering completion retains the existing paid-funding, independent-demand and capacity requirements. This change adds no time expiry or financial permission.

During dispatch, the helper supplies the frozen coin/account to the existing confirmation restore helpers and the original business API. It then restores the player's latest coin, active wallet and order fields when those selections remain valid. An archived coin or wallet no longer legally held is cleared. Completing or aborting one offering preserves a different pending offering only while it remains a live preparation controlled by that actor.

The nine `CharterTargetScriptTest` cases execute the actual candidate event, context effects and predicates with a deliberately limited Java script interpreter. They also execute the existing production confirmation helpers, supply-freeze effect, project-cash effect, finance-transfer effect and their required trigger/value subset. All nine action routes are checked for binding to A while B is currently selected. Cases include changed controller/house head, completed or failed offering state, archived or missing coin, frozen/replaced/foreign primary account, changed issuer/maker account, simultaneous windows, replay, stale displays, changed reviewed supply, and restoration of current selections/pending projects.

The account-selection and ensure-account calls are explicit fixture ports returning authored existing accounts. Completion, abort, promotion, failed-supply cancellation, unused-reserve release, bond restoration and maker restart are explicit dispatch ports; they do not execute those production business effects. The completion/abort ports reproduce their unconditional removal of `xfm_pending_offer` to exercise the real restoration hazard. Unknown effect, trigger and arithmetic operators fail the interpreter. This is software regression coverage, not CK3 engine, save/reload or complete business acceptance. Native event-context persistence and normal GUI operation remain live acceptance work.
