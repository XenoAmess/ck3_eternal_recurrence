# Episode 1 final English subtitle script v3: Who receives the benefits of a holy site?

[Production note, not spoken: exact ordered English text paired with the approved Chinese narration. The final faith-withdrawal action was not recorded; native state readback and a post-action gameplay still are used. This is a text source, not listening or full-speed viewing approval.]

## 一千虔诚买到了什么

<!-- cue: V3-N001 -->
We spend 1,000 piety to adopt Najaf. It is only an ordinary holy site for us, and the relic slots are still empty.

<!-- cue: V3-N002 -->
What did that 1,000 actually buy? If we already rule this land, why pay again? If adoption does not immediately add a long list of bonuses, was the money wasted?

<!-- cue: V3-N003 -->
Start with the clearest change: our faith now recognizes Najaf as a holy site. It joins our list. But the land has not changed hands, the site has not become major, the empty slots have not filled themselves, and the nearby special building has not been built for us.

<!-- cue: V3-N004 -->
That 1,000 buys Najaf a place in our faith's network, and the option to develop it further. Its value depends on what we want: personal Learning, development in a vassal's county, political influence across the faith, or access to a particular building. Those goals can lead to very different answers.

<!-- cue: V3-N005 -->
That is the question we will play through today. First, who receives a holy site's benefits? Then, should a major slot go to Najaf, Mecca, or a Site of Wisdom? Finally, we will treat relics as assets with real owners and alternative uses, and ask whose property enshrinement, replacement, retrieval and withdrawal actually move.

<!-- cue: V3-N006 -->
We use separate saves prepared for these mechanism tests. The character is given the Maliki rite. The initial adoption branch receives 2,000 extra piety; the branch demonstrating more actions receives 5,000 extra in total. Adding piety also raised the Level of Devotion. None of this was earned through ordinary play.

<!-- cue: V3-N007 -->
We reload different branches between chapters. Balances form one continuous account only within the same sequence.

<!-- cue: V3-N008 -->
What this episode really wants to answer is the question before clicking: why should I do this?

## 普通圣地，也有它该做的事

<!-- cue: V3-N009 -->
It is easy to read ordinary and major as lower and higher tiers, and assume the higher tier gives more of everything. But holy site effects come in layers.

<!-- cue: V3-N010 -->
First, the local layer: some effects benefit a local character, others the county itself. These may already exist at ordinary status. Major status adds another set of effects across the faith, with another set of eligibility conditions. Promotion does not award the local layer a second time.

<!-- cue: V3-N011 -->
Take Najaf. Its fixed local effect is +3 Clergy Opinion. The county also receives a development growth multiplier and Popular Opinion that scale with effective relic rarity. Here, the county holder is our vassal. The emperor clicking Adopt does not make all those local benefits the emperor's personal stats.

<!-- cue: V3-N012 -->
Development in a vassal's land can indirectly help the emperor. But that depends on who holds the holdings, how taxes are paid and what the contract says. Extra income in a county does not add the same amount directly to the imperial purse. A good investment in a vassal may not be the best investment in ourselves right now.

<!-- cue: V3-N013 -->
Now consider the Site of Wisdom we created in Samarra. It is still ordinary, but the player holds the county. That better matches personal growth and developing our own land than Najaf under a vassal. Wisdom's local layer gives the county holder +2 Learning. County construction costs in gold, prestige and piety can all fall with effective rarity, and development growth also has an additive term.

<!-- cue: V3-N014 -->
From here on, I will call the sum of the site's currently effective relic rarity its effective total. This is not the number of relics. Nor does seeing one rarity-four item mean the total will always be four.

<!-- cue: V3-N015 -->
Each point of effective total reduces Wisdom's three construction costs by 1% each and adds 0.01 to development growth. At a hypothetical total of four, that means -4% to each cost and +0.04 development growth. That last term is additive development growth, not another +4% development growth multiplier.

<!-- cue: V3-N016 -->
The corresponding development effects at Najaf and Mecca use multipliers. Both concern development, but the numbers do not mean the same thing.

<!-- cue: V3-N017 -->
This is also an easy place to pay twice for the wrong reason. If all you want is Wisdom's local +2 Learning, promotion to major is not a necessary purchase. Those two points are already in the local layer. Check separately what promotion adds.

<!-- cue: V3-N018 -->
These are the roles defined by the holy site types. Applying them to the current character and county still requires their respective conditions. A description alone does not prove a net increase in the character's stats.

<!-- cue: V3-L018-01 -->
After Najaf becomes a major holy site, all three slots are still empty and the site remains inactive. Raising its status and enshrining a relic solve different problems.

<!-- cue: V3-L018-02 -->
Next, we enshrine the rarity-four cloak. The effective relic total rises from zero to four, with no further piety deducted. We can confirm the enshrinement succeeded, but cannot credit it with every stat change.

<!-- cue: V3-L018-03 -->
Empty slots do not necessarily mean no benefits at all, and an active site does not mean everyone receives every bonus. First check the county holder's faith and whether the site is major for the relevant faith, then the benefit you actually need.

## 主要名额，交换的是哪组收益

<!-- cue: V3-N019 -->
Now consider major status. Najaf's major layer has a clear focus: each eligible recipient gains one Diplomacy per level of their own Devotion. It also provides +5 Clergy Opinion and a monthly piety multiplier based on the effective total.

<!-- cue: V3-N020 -->
Each point of effective total contributes +1% monthly piety. At four, the underlying multiplier term is +4%. Diplomacy instead uses that recipient's own Level of Devotion. For a character at level four, the underlying Diplomacy contribution is +4. We cannot use the emperor's level for every character in the faith.

<!-- cue: V3-N021 -->
Najaf therefore has a clear use when you want Diplomacy, better clergy relations and piety income. But adopting it as ordinary does not deliver that entire major package. Benefits requiring major status also need a major slot in the plan.

<!-- cue: V3-N022 -->
A major Site of Wisdom offers one Diplomacy per Level of Devotion, plus Learning and Diplomacy lifestyle experience multipliers. Each experience multiplier gains 1% per point of effective total, or +4% each at four. The local +2 Learning remains the original local effect; major status does not give it again.

<!-- cue: V3-N023 -->
Here is a concrete comparison. Suppose we can keep only one of these sites major, and move the slot from Najaf to Wisdom. Both provide one Diplomacy per Level of Devotion. The net change in that underlying coefficient is zero.

<!-- cue: V3-N024 -->
We cannot count Wisdom's entire Diplomacy entry as a new gain while forgetting the Najaf contribution we lose. The real exchange is Najaf's Clergy Opinion and monthly piety for Wisdom's two lifestyle experience bonuses. Their effective totals may also differ, so calculate each site separately.

<!-- cue: V3-N025 -->
If you plan to pursue a Learning or Diplomacy lifestyle and the ruler has many years left to learn, the experience bonuses may be worth considering. If clergy relations are holding the realm together, or piety income is tight, losing Najaf has a cost. The better choice depends on the character's next goals and how long they can benefit.

<!-- cue: V3-N026 -->
Mecca should not become our default sacrifice simply because it is convenient to demonstrate. Its major effects provide a 2% monthly piety multiplier per knight. Knight effectiveness and piety level impact each gain 1% per point of the site's effective rarity total. Piety level impact amplifies the modifiers already provided by your current level of devotion. Check the effects listed for that level: this does not directly award piety or devotion experience, or raise the level itself.

<!-- cue: V3-N027 -->
Let us use a hypothetical example. An eligible character has ten knights, contributing 2% each: that gives an underlying monthly piety multiplier of +20%. This does not mean our filmed character has ten knights, or that the final monthly piety panel must show a net 20% increase.

<!-- cue: V3-N028 -->
The other two effects use Mecca's own effective total. Do not simply borrow Najaf's four points. For someone planning knight-led warfare with many knights, demoting Mecca may give up a particularly useful package.

<!-- cue: V3-N029 -->
When comparing major slots, first ask what the old site does and for whom, then ask the same of the new site. Having enough capacity is a question of feasibility, not whether the change is worthwhile.

<!-- cue: V3-N029A -->
As for additional abilities, the three types compared here—Mecca, Najaf, and the Site of Wisdom—declare no extra special-ability parameters. Promoting them to major adds the respective major modifiers we have just discussed. Check other types individually; do not generalize these three examples to every holy site.

## 激活、信仰与受益者，别混成一个开关

<!-- cue: V3-N030 -->
One more issue can change the whole comparison: several faiths may recognize the same holy site. Each faith's major status is stored separately. That does not mean each faith has an entirely separate activation state for the site.

<!-- cue: V3-N031 -->
The holy site itself has one global active state and effective rarity total. Which faith recognizes a relic is a separate question. Who actually receives a particular set of effects depends on that set's own conditions.

<!-- cue: V3-N032 -->
The faith-wide layer has a crucial gate. The local county holder's current faith must match the faith receiving those benefits, and that faith must also treat the site as major.

<!-- cue: V3-N033 -->
It checks the county holder's personal faith right now. Not the color of the great empire on the map, not the top liege's faith, and not the faith of the county's population.

<!-- cue: V3-N034 -->
Two faiths sharing a holy site do not automatically receive a full faith-wide package each, even if both consider it major. When the county holder belongs to one, the other cannot bypass that gate merely by recognizing the relics. A later conversion by the holder may change the calculation.

<!-- cue: V3-N035 -->
Active status is not one master switch for every effect. Local fixed effects and county scaling effects have their own activation requirements. Faith-wide fixed effects do not simply reuse that same switch: they check major status, the county holder's faith and other conditions. Rarity-scaled effects must also consider the effective total.

<!-- cue: V3-N036 -->
Inactive does not automatically mean every fixed benefit becomes zero. Active does not mean every character in every associated faith receives every bonus, either. The interface hiding part of a description is not exactly the same as how a stat is calculated.

<!-- cue: V3-N037 -->
You do not need to memorize every detail. Ask three simple questions: which layer am I looking at, what conditions does it require, and who benefits? That keeps you from counting benefits you cannot receive, or treating potentially remaining fixed effects as zero when comparing major slots.

## 两千实付，和三千预算

<!-- cue: V3-N038 -->
Back to the rank changes we demonstrated. We switch to a test branch where Najaf has already been adopted and 4,250 piety remains. With all major slots occupied, we first demote Mecca to ordinary for 1,000, then promote Najaf for another 1,000. The balance is 2,250.

<!-- cue: V3-N039 -->
This branch actually spent 2,000. Mecca remains on the holy site list; it only yields a major slot. The total number of holy sites does not fall. The major count drops by one, then Najaf fills the slot. Restoring the count does not restore Mecca's major effects, or refund the 2,000 piety.

<!-- cue: V3-N040 -->
If planning from before Najaf's adoption, with major slots already full, all three current quotes still at 1,000 and no free actions, the complete budget is 3,000: adopt for 1,000, demote the old site for 1,000, then promote the new one for 1,000. This is a conditional plan, not a 3,000-piety bill manufactured by joining different saves.

<!-- cue: V3-N041 -->
If you already paid for adoption, compare the additional investment from here. That fee is spent. Regretting the first 1,000 is no reason to ignore whether the next 2,000 is worthwhile. If you have not paid yet, preparing just 1,000 does not buy the whole major package.

<!-- cue: V3-N042 -->
Free actions change that calculation. In this version, creating a new faith grants a limited pool of three actions shared by the faith. A new ruler does not receive another set. Using the last free action may make the next step charge again. The free allowance has an opportunity cost too.

<!-- cue: V3-N043 -->
Adoption, promotion, demotion and withdrawal each have their own decision cooldowns; creation has a separate restriction. Equal durations do not mean one shared lock for every button. Our demonstration allowed a first promotion after demotion, but that does not mean we could immediately demote another site. For a chain of actions, check whether each completed step leaves the next one possible.

<!-- cue: V3-N044 -->
Religious politics also belongs in the budget. Under this version's rules, if the county holder is not the actor, promotion gives that holder +50 opinion of the actor, while demotion gives -50. These modifiers last ten years and decay. There are also modifiers tied to vassal stances: promotion lists +30 for same-faith Zealot vassals; demotion lists -20 each for Zealot and Parochial vassals.

<!-- cue: V3-N045 -->
Consider those alongside other relationship effects. These are vassal stances, not a reason to include everyone with a similarly named trait.

<!-- cue: V3-N046 -->
Demoting someone's holy site also tells its holder that their land is no longer central to our faith's network. For a ruler trying to appease vassals, that may deserve more thought than the visible 1,000-piety price.

## 先看看自己已有的巴格达

<!-- cue: V3-N047 -->
If my goal is the emperor's personal Learning and long-term development, there is an easily forgotten alternative before adopting Najaf: invest in the holy sites we already have.

<!-- cue: V3-N048 -->
Our Sunni faith already recognizes Baghdad, so we cannot pay to adopt it again. In the earlier inspection, the player held it and it already had active relics. Baghdad's local type effects already include Learning and development benefits.

<!-- cue: V3-N049 -->
That does not prove promoting Baghdad is optimal. Its latest state, capacity and price still need checking. But it should at least enter the comparison, rather than disappear from the plan because Najaf has the more eye-catching name.

<!-- cue: V3-N050 -->
For the same piety budget, compare three paths: use an existing site better, adopt another location, or create the type you need. Also consider keeping the resources for something more urgent. Comparing those alternatives is how we choose a plan.

<!-- cue: V3-N051 -->
Special buildings should not be bundled into adoption's benefits. Different buildings use different religious conditions: some require both conditions, while others accept either one.

<!-- cue: V3-N052 -->
The relevant conditions for the Imam Ali Mosque require a holy site of the holding owner's faith, and also the Islamic or syncretism condition. If the building already exists and only the holy site association is missing, adoption may fill that gap. But check the actual holding owner and whether the building is enabled.

<!-- cue: V3-N053 -->
The Great Mosque of Samarra instead has alternatives: the Islamic or syncretism branch, or holy site status for the holding owner's faith. For a Sunni holder already meeting the Islamic branch, creating a Site of Wisdom is not a necessary unlocking cost. And a building that has not been built will not simply appear when we click Adopt.

<!-- cue: V3-N054 -->
Pilgrimage has its own calculation. An associated holy site may offer another destination worth considering, but the host, activity type, recent visits, route, travel costs and safety still matter. The Hajj still goes to Mecca; a Site of Wisdom cannot replace it. For non-adventurers, ordinary pilgrimage also excludes the current capital. If Samarra is your capital, check that first.

<!-- cue: V3-N055 -->
Adoption might serve a building or a pilgrimage. Name the actual goal, so you know whether you bought a condition you need or an opportunity you will not use.

## 同一座圣地，只有一份圣物库存

<!-- cue: V3-N056 -->
Next, bring relics into this calculation.

<!-- cue: V3-N057 -->
We enshrined the Prophet Muhammad's cloak at Najaf. It moved from the character to the holy site, appeared in its slot, and the site showed active with an effective total of four. The first change was ownership. We cannot count it as a holy site investment while also crediting the emperor with its full personal equipment value.

<!-- cue: V3-N058 -->
If another faith recognizes Najaf, it sees the same place, physical inventory and slots. Enshrining one relic does not create a second copy for that other faith.

<!-- cue: V3-N059 -->
What is stored separately is each faith's association with the site, its ordinary or major status, and which faith recognizes each item. A shared inventory does not mean shared recognition. Shared recognition does not make all the conditions for faith-wide benefits identical either.

<!-- cue: V3-N060 -->
Effective rarity also depends on the county holder. Their current faith must still be associated with the site. Its recognition is then used to evaluate the relics actually equipped in the slots. An item in inventory is not necessarily in a slot that counts. High item rarity does not necessarily mean a high current contribution to this holy site.

<!-- cue: V3-N061 -->
Here is a hypothetical calculation from the rules. The holder follows faith A. The old relic has rarity four but A does not recognize it. The new relic has rarity three, but A recognizes it and all slot and enshrinement conditions are met. In item rarity, that is four for three. For the current holder's faith, the effective contribution could rise from zero to three.

<!-- cue: V3-N062 -->
That is why quality color alone is not enough. Conversely, if A already recognizes the rarity-four relic, replacing it with a recognized rarity-three relic could reduce the current contribution from four to three. Recognition by more faiths may make a relic more resilient to later conversions or changes of holder. It does not automatically make it stronger now.

<!-- cue: V3-N063 -->
The holder's faith must also remain associated with the site. If the holder converts to a faith that does not recognize this as a holy site, relics staying in the slots do not preserve their previous effective contribution.

<!-- cue: V3-N064 -->
The real comparison is between the relic's previous use, its contribution now, and how control of the location might change. The rarer the relic, the more reason to consider keeping it, enshrining it elsewhere, or leaving it where it is for now.

## 直接替换，和先取出来，不是一回事

<!-- cue: V3-N065 -->
A shared inventory means changing relics here also changes an asset shared with other associated faiths. Automatic slot selection will not weigh those interests for you.

<!-- cue: V3-N066 -->
It looks for a compatible empty slot first. If none is available, it chooses a compatible old item with lower item rarity, breaking ties by the existing order. It does not compare who recognizes each relic, which faith might object to its removal, or the opportunity cost of personal equipment.

<!-- cue: V3-N067 -->
The base game can replace an old relic directly within one enshrinement action. With a valid acting character, the new relic enters the holy site and the old one goes to that actor. It does not automatically return to its original donor, another faith's leader or the county holder. With a separate retrieval, the normal recipient is also the current actor.

<!-- cue: V3-N068 -->
Replacement still needs the original requirements for enshrining the new relic and removing the old one. With a shared, occupied slot, identify exactly which item will leave. Afterward, check where both items actually went.

<!-- cue: V3-L068-01 -->
Now we replace the cloak directly with another rarity-four relic. The new item enters Najaf, and the old cloak returns to the emperor performing the action, not the local county holder.

<!-- cue: V3-L068-02 -->
The effective total stays at four, and piety is unchanged this time. But the preview also lists changes to other people's opinions. We need to consider those as well as the piety cost.

<!-- cue: V3-L068-03 -->
The returned cloak can be used elsewhere, while the incoming relic gives up its previous personal use. Compare both uses and each faith's recognition before deciding whether the swap is worthwhile.

<!-- cue: V3-N069 -->
We have already filmed a separate retrieval of the cloak, which actually cost 500 piety. It returned to the character, the slot became empty and the active state changed. But that 500 is the observed purse change for that retrieval. It does not cover every form of replacement, nor does it mean we fully read the character's devotion experience.

<!-- cue: V3-N070 -->
Direct replacement may apply both the new relic's enshrinement rules and the old relic's removal rules. Retrieving first and then enshrining is therefore not necessarily the same bill as a direct replacement.

<!-- cue: V3-N071 -->
Consider another conditional example: the old relic has rarity four, the new one rarity three, the actor's faith recognizes both, and the relevant branch conditions remain satisfied. The base value is 500 for rarity four and 250 for rarity three. The enshrinement portion may apply a -250 change, while removal may cost another 500 piety and 500 devotion experience.

<!-- cue: V3-N072 -->
Together, that could mean losing 750 from the purse and 500 devotion experience.

<!-- cue: V3-N073 -->
This is a conditional example, not a universal 750 charge for every four-for-three replacement. In your own game, expand the complete current preview and check piety, devotion and relationships separately. A better incoming item does not mean the action has only benefits.

<!-- cue: V3-N074 -->
Other faiths may react too. Losing no piety yourself does not mean nobody objects. The opinion effects target vassals, lieges and religious leaders selected by the rules. They are not a uniform adjustment to every character of that faith worldwide.

<!-- cue: V3-N075 -->
A message about reclaiming a relic does not prove someone already has a usable casus belli. The normal case also checks whether the attacker recognizes it, whether the holder's faith does not, whether the item can transfer, and the usual war declaration conditions. If both faiths recognize the old relic, that message alone does not establish the normal casus belli.

<!-- cue: V3-N076 -->
A replacement that seems cheaper in piety may therefore put a disputed item in your inventory. The real bargain must include its later diplomatic consequences.

## 退出圣地网络，东西会退给谁

<!-- cue: V3-N077 -->
If we later decide not to develop Najaf, withdrawal is not a one-click undo.

<!-- cue: V3-N078 -->
Demotion only gives up major status; the site stays on our faith's list. Withdrawal removes our faith's association with it. This version's normal decision targets ordinary sites and also checks minimum counts and other requirements. Total holy site capacity and major capacity are different questions.

<!-- cue: V3-N079 -->
In another reloaded test branch, we withdrew from ordinary Najaf with empty slots. It left our faith's list, while another faith still treated it as major and the location itself remained. That demonstrates removal of an association, not what happens to relics in an occupied inventory.

<!-- cue: V3-N080 -->
If relics are present, there are two different cases.

<!-- cue: V3-N081 -->
As long as any other faith remains associated, withdrawing our side does not run that specific relic return process. It checks whether another associated faith exists, not how many adherents it has left on the map. Being the donor does not mean the relics automatically come back to me on withdrawal.

<!-- cue: V3-N082 -->
Only when the last associated faith withdraws, and the local county has a holder, do this version's rules hand the enshrined items to that county holder. The holder need not be the actor, share the actor's faith, or even recognize those relics.

<!-- cue: V3-N083 -->
That differs from normal retrieval, which gives the item to the current actor. If the county has no holder, we cannot invent a default recipient. The ownership change on withdrawal follows different fee rules from explicit retrieval. That does not rule out other ownership events or political consequences.

<!-- cue: V3-L083-01 -->
Next, we withdraw from shared Baghdad. We spend 1,000 piety and it disappears from our faith's list, but the relic remains in its original slot.

<!-- cue: V3-L083-02 -->
Here we need extra care: the holder still follows our faith, but our faith has withdrawn. Under the current conditions, the site can no longer activate; the old state shown on screen has not caught up yet.

<!-- cue: V3-L083-03 -->
Sharing a site does not mean each faith independently receives its full benefits. Before withdrawing, check who remains associated, the holder's faith, and who ultimately keeps the relics.

<!-- cue: V3-L083-04 -->
Now consider the wisdom site associated only with our faith. After the last faith withdraws, its shared inventory is emptied. The cloak returns to the emperor's personal inventory, without being automatically equipped.

<!-- cue: V3-L083-05 -->
Here, the rule returns the relics to the local county holder. This time the emperor happens to hold the county himself. If someone else holds the land, abandoning the site is not a way to retrieve its relics for yourself.

<!-- cue: V3-N084 -->
These return rules are specific to the 1.20.0.4 version used in this episode. When changing versions, do not carry over assumptions about who ends up with the items.

## 新建一处，是把目标放在哪里

<!-- cue: V3-N085 -->
Finally, return to the Site of Wisdom. Adoption connects us to an existing location and type. Creation lets us choose the type and location we need, but also requires the corresponding resources and an eligible relic.

<!-- cue: V3-N086 -->
We reload another test branch here. Najaf is already adopted and we start with 4,250 piety. Withdrawing from empty Najaf costs 1,000; creating a Site of Wisdom in Samarra then costs 2,000, leaving 1,250. Do not join this balance to the earlier cloak retrieval sequence.

<!-- cue: V3-N087 -->
Withdrawal followed by creation is the route demonstrated here. It does not mean creation always requires withdrawing first.

<!-- cue: V3-N088 -->
Our chosen type, location and relic produced an ordinary Site of Wisdom. The cloak entered its slot; the site read active with an effective total of four, and the player held the county. Creation did not also grant major status. Nor did it prove every local modifier had been added as a net gain to the character and county panels.

<!-- cue: V3-N089 -->
For our goals, it places a local type focused on Learning and construction somewhere closer to the player. The cost is 2,000 piety, the relic's alternative personal use, and network and location capacity. If we also want the major layer's Diplomacy and two experience bonuses, that needs a separate major-slot comparison. Do not count them as benefits already purchased by ordinary creation.

<!-- cue: V3-N090 -->
Now look back at the opening 1,000. Whether it was wasted depends on what we lacked and what adoption supplied, not whether our list gained an attractive name.

<!-- cue: V3-N091 -->
For personal Learning, first examine existing Baghdad and the counties you hold. For religious politics, compare Najaf's fixed effects and monthly piety. For knight-led warfare, think carefully before giving up Mecca. For lifestyles, calculate Wisdom's major experience bonuses without counting the shared Diplomacy coefficient again.

<!-- cue: V3-N092 -->
When using relics, check the single shared inventory, the holder's faith and recognition. When withdrawing, check associations, recipients and the current version. Compare the full follow-up investment, the old benefits you surrender and who may object. Then decide whether to adopt, promote, create, or keep the resources for now.

<!-- cue: V3-N093 -->
A holy site's value has to fit a specific person, a specific place and what you actually want to do next.
