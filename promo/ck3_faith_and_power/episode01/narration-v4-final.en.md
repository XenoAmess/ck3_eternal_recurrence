# Are holy sites worth the investment?

> v4 English subtitle script. All 115 narration cues have been measured; encoded film duration and final chapter times are still pending.

## What do you want the investment to do?

<!-- V4-C01-01 -->
Have you ever wanted to add someone else's holy site to your own faith's list? You spend the Piety, add the name, then discover that the relic slots are still empty and the site has not become Eminent. What did that investment actually buy?

<!-- V4-C01-02 -->
Adopting a holy site changes your faith's connection to that place. It does not transfer the land, build a special building, copy someone else's relics, or give your faith the site's Eminent status in another faith.

<!-- V4-C01-03 -->
Start with what you need. More Learning for your ruler? Development in your domain? Support from the clergy? Long-term bonuses for your faith's adherents? Each goal depends on different conditions.

<!-- V4-C01-04 -->
Today we will connect those questions. What do ordinary and Eminent status each provide? Why can the same relics produce a different result under another holder? Can someone else take what I enshrine? And who gets it when my faith abandons the site?

<!-- V4-C01-05 -->
The demonstrations use a separate mechanics experiment based on the Abbasids in 867. The character was prepared with the Maliki rite and a total of 5,000 extra Piety. This also raised Devotion and the Level of Devotion. These resources were not earned through normal play.

<!-- V4-C01-06 -->
The Nadjaf and Baghdad demonstrations follow one branch. Creating the Site of Wisdom and finally abandoning it follow another, reloaded branch. We track balances within each branch, rather than combining different experiments into one account.

<!-- V4-C01-07 -->
The County holder is the character who actually holds the County containing the holy site. That may be the emperor or a vassal. Start with this person when checking conditions and benefits, rather than looking only at the top liege.

<!-- V4-C01-08 -->
Faith and Religion are distinct. Several faiths can share one holy site. When checking Eminent status or whether a relic is recognized, belonging to the same religion does not make those faiths interchangeable.

## Who receives the benefit?

<!-- V4-C02-01 -->
It is tempting to treat ordinary and Eminent as lower and higher levels, and assume every benefit improves with an upgrade. Separate local modifiers from modifiers for the entire faith first. Otherwise, you may pay again for an effect that was already available.

<!-- V4-C02-02 -->
First, consider the attribute modifiers supplied directly by a holy site type. Among these, the modifiers for all characters of the faith require Eminent status, both fixed and scaled by relic rarity. Local modifiers, special buildings, and special abilities each have their own conditions.

<!-- V4-C02-03 -->
For those modifiers to be produced, the faith must designate the site as Eminent, and the County holder must currently follow that faith. The holder matters: not their top liege, the color of the realm, or the faith of the local population.

<!-- V4-C02-04 -->
Suppose Faiths A and B both designate the site as Eminent, but its County holder follows A. Even if B recognizes the relics in its slots, that does not bypass the holder condition and grant every follower of B the full set of bonuses. A later conversion could change the conditions.

<!-- V4-C02-05 -->
Local character modifiers and County modifiers have their own conditions. Ordinary holy sites can offer local benefits. Local character effects also depend on the recipient's title ownership and faith; they do not automatically go to everyone nearby, or everyone of that faith.

<!-- V4-C02-06 -->
An inactive holy site does not necessarily provide no fixed bonuses at all. Fixed modifiers for the entire faith still require Eminent status and the County holder's faith to match, but do not impose that active flag as another universal check. Local modifiers have separate activation conditions; scaled effects also depend on the total they read.

<!-- V4-C02-07 -->
The reverse is also true. An active icon does not prove that every character of every associated faith has received every benefit. Especially while paused, current conditions, cached results, and the modifiers already aggregated for a character may not have updated together.

<!-- V4-C02-08 -->
When comparing options, identify the effect you actually want. Is it local, or for the entire faith? Who receives it? Does it require Eminent status? Answer those questions before committing more resources, rather than judging only by an upgraded label.

## What does a Site of Wisdom actually add?

<!-- V4-C03-01 -->
Take the Site of Wisdom we created in Samarra. This is a type of holy site, not a separate character attribute. The new site is still ordinary. Creating it did not include Eminent status.

<!-- V4-C03-02 -->
Its fixed local character modifier includes +2 Learning. Its County modifiers affect construction costs paid in Gold, Prestige, or Piety, as well as Development Growth. If those local benefits are your goal, elevation is not a necessary purchase.

<!-- V4-C03-03 -->
Under the type's rules, each point of qualifying relic rarity reduces each of the three construction costs by 1%, and adds 0.01 to Development Growth. A total of four therefore corresponds to -4% for each cost and +0.04 Development Growth.

<!-- V4-C03-04 -->
That +0.04 is an additive modifier, not a 4% increase to the Development Growth multiplier. The interface labels can look similar, but the calculations are different. The longer your construction plan, the more that distinction matters.

<!-- V4-C03-05 -->
Making the Site of Wisdom Eminent adds a separate set of modifiers for the entire faith: one Diplomacy per Level of Devotion, plus Learning and Diplomacy Lifestyle Experience multipliers scaled by the relic rarity total. It does not grant the local +2 Learning a second time.

<!-- V4-C03-06 -->
With a rarity total of four, each Lifestyle Experience multiplier contributes +4%. The Diplomacy bonus uses each recipient's own Level of Devotion. An emperor at level four does not make every other follower count as level four.

<!-- V4-C03-07 -->
An Eminent Site of Wisdom may appeal to a young ruler planning a long Learning or Diplomacy lifestyle. If you only want better construction in your domain, compare the ordinary site's local benefits first. For an elderly ruler, consider how much time remains to use the experience bonus.

<!-- V4-C03-08 -->
Location is part of the decision. Putting a useful type in a County you manage yourself is a different plan from leaving its local benefits in a vassal's hands. Both can be valuable, but a vassal's benefits cannot simply be copied into the emperor's own income.

## What are you giving up by changing the major sites?

<!-- V4-C04-01 -->
Nadjaf's Eminent modifiers include one Diplomacy per Level of Devotion, +5 Clergy Opinion, and a Monthly Piety multiplier scaled by the relic rarity total. Its local effects separately include +3 Clergy Opinion, along with Development and Popular Opinion modifiers for the County.

<!-- V4-C04-02 -->
At a qualifying rarity total of four, Nadjaf's Monthly Piety multiplier contributes +4%. That is separate from its Diplomacy and Clergy Opinion modifiers. One number alone does not measure the value of the whole site.

<!-- V4-C04-03 -->
Suppose you can keep only one of these sites Eminent. You demote Nadjaf and elevate the Site of Wisdom. Both offer one Diplomacy per Level of Devotion. If the recipient conditions are otherwise the same, swapping those identical coefficients does not add another copy of the bonus.

<!-- V4-C04-04 -->
The real exchange is Nadjaf's Clergy Opinion and Monthly Piety for the Site of Wisdom's two Lifestyle Experience multipliers. The two sites may also have different rarity totals, so calculate them separately. Assess what the old site loses as well as what the new one gains.

<!-- V4-C04-05 -->
If Piety is scarce and you need support from the clergy, Nadjaf's combination may fit better. If you are developing Learning and Diplomacy over a long reign, consider the Site of Wisdom. There is no best choice independent of the character's situation, and a new type is not automatically worth switching to.

<!-- V4-C04-06 -->
Mecca should not become the default sacrifice just because it is convenient for a demonstration. Its Eminent modifiers include Monthly Piety per Knight, plus Knight Effectiveness and Level of Devotion Impact scaled by the relic rarity total.

<!-- V4-C04-07 -->
At 2% per Knight, ten Knights contribute a +20% Monthly Piety modifier. It is calculated alongside other income modifiers. To determine the final monthly gain, include it in the character's overall income calculation.

<!-- V4-C04-08 -->
Level of Devotion Impact amplifies modifiers already provided by the character's current Level of Devotion. It does not directly add Piety, add progress toward the next level, or raise the level itself. If Knights are central to your military plans, include these potential losses in the comparison.

<!-- V4-C04-09 -->
Before reallocating an Eminent slot, ask what the old site does, and for whom; then ask the same of the new one. An adoption fee already paid is a sunk cost. It is no reason to keep paying for a plan that no longer suits your situation.

## Special effects and the two separate limits

<!-- V4-C05-01 -->
Eminent status is not required for every special ability. Mecca, Nadjaf, and the Site of Wisdom have no extra ability parameters in the types compared here. Those three examples do not establish that ordinary holy sites can never have special functions.

<!-- V4-C05-02 -->
Jerusalem is a counterexample. The relevant conversion rules check a particular holy site parameter, along with association, control, location, and other conditions. Those branches do not require Eminent status. A type parameter and an Eminent attribute modifier are separate things.

<!-- V4-C05-03 -->
Ordinary status does not make the ability unconditional, either. The relevant checks still consult the site's cached active state. Some also check the County holder's faith, a fixed location, and the context of a Court Chaplain task. Finding a parameter does not prove that its benefit has been applied.

<!-- V4-C05-04 -->
Elevation cannot invent a special ability absent from the type. If a type does define a parameter, examine what actually uses it. Start with the task you want to accomplish and work back to its conditions, rather than spending for the grade's name alone.

<!-- V4-C05-04A -->
Eminent status also matters in other systems. Specific rules distinguish ordinary and Eminent sites for pilgrimage rewards, travel visits, and part of the cost of establishing a Spiritual Head of Faith. Check the activities and decisions you plan to use, rather than comparing attribute tables alone.

<!-- V4-C05-05 -->
Now consider the limits. Under the normal rules of this base-game version and its installed expansions, a faith can have at most nine holy sites, of which at most three can be Eminent. Eminent sites count toward the total; they are not three extra sites on top of nine ordinary ones. Starting counts and maximum counts are different things.

<!-- V4-C05-06 -->
We also checked the stock decisions, events, and effects, and found no route in normal play that raises either maximum. Normal adoption, creation, and elevation all check the relevant count. Free actions waive the corresponding cost; they do not add total capacity or Eminent slots.

<!-- V4-C05-07 -->
Editing a list or save, or using a mod to exceed a limit, is different from the normal rules raising that limit. More sites in an old save do not prove that another normal addition is allowed. This finding is limited to the current stock content; recheck it after changing versions or mods.

<!-- V4-C05-08 -->
Adoption and creation need room under the total holy site limit. Elevating an ordinary site also needs an Eminent slot. Demotion frees an Eminent slot without reducing the total. Abandonment removes your faith's association with the site, subject to minimum counts and other requirements.

<!-- V4-C05-09 -->
Location is a third, separate constraint. Having room does not mean this site can be adopted, or any type can be created here. Authority, type, materials, existing sites, and current requirements still apply. Available capacity is only one part of a workable plan.

## One site, several faiths

<!-- V4-C06-01 -->
Now look at the actual Nadjaf. Several faiths recognize the same place; they do not each have a hidden copy beneath it. The location, slots, and relic inventory are shared. A relic enshrined by one faith is not duplicated for the others.

<!-- V4-C06-02 -->
What is stored separately is each faith's association with the site and whether it regards the site as ordinary or Eminent. It can be Eminent for one faith and ordinary for another. Each relic's recognition by each faith is another, separate check.

<!-- V4-C06-03 -->
Sharing therefore means neither a separate inventory for every faith nor identical recognition by every faith. The site and its assets are shared; association, status, and recognition must still be checked separately.

<!-- V4-C06-04 -->
In this campaign, five faiths were associated with Baghdad. Sunni and one other faith recognized the same enshrined relic; the other three did not. The item and slot were unchanged. Only the faith evaluating it changed, and so did the answer.

<!-- V4-C06-05 -->
Both faiths in the Nadjaf comparison recognized both of these rarity 4 relics. Swapping them did not change recognition for either faith. If improved recognition is your goal, find candidates that actually differ; a new item name alone is not a new trade-off.

<!-- V4-C06-06 -->
Now add the benefit conditions back in. Recognition answers whether that faith recognizes that relic. It does not bypass an Eminent requirement, the County holder's faith, or the rules determining which character receives an effect.

<!-- V4-C06-07 -->
For a shared site, first identify the County holder and their faith. Then check each faith's association and its recognition of each item. Asking only whether everyone recognizes the site can miss the person whose faith determines the current relic calculation and eligibility for benefits.

<!-- V4-C06-08 -->
Sharing also creates future exposure. A vassal's conversion, a new holder after succession, or another party managing the site may change which items fit and who can benefit. Before enshrining something valuable for the long term, consider how long you expect to retain control.

## How the rarity total is calculated

<!-- V4-C07-01 -->
The official interface field is Total Artifact Rarity Levels. We will calculate it slot by slot. It is not a relic count, nor the sum of every item in the inventory. Only slotted relics that meet the current conditions contribute to the total.

<!-- V4-C07-02 -->
Start with the County holder's current faith. It must still regard this place as a holy site before the calculation proceeds to the slotted relics. If the holder's faith has abandoned the site, items remaining there do not preserve the previous total.

<!-- V4-C07-03 -->
Then check each slot. Empty slots contribute nothing. For an occupied slot, the holder's faith must recognize the item before its own rarity level is added. Merely belonging to the site's inventory without occupying a slot does not make an item contribute.

<!-- V4-C07-04 -->
Here is a rule-based example. Slot one holds a rarity 4 item recognized by Faith A. Slot two holds a rarity 3 item A does not recognize. Slot three holds a recognized rarity 2 item. If the holder follows A and A still regards this as a holy site, the contributions are four, zero, and two: a total of six.

<!-- V4-C07-05 -->
Now suppose a new holder follows Faith B. B is also associated with the site, but recognizes only the second and third items. Without changing the inventory, the current calculation becomes zero, three, and two: a total of five. This is a conditional comparison, not a conversion observed in this episode.

<!-- V4-C07-06 -->
Now compare two candidates. The current holder's faith does not recognize the old rarity 4 item, but does recognize the new rarity 3 item. If all other requirements are met, replacing it can raise that slot's contribution from zero to three. Lower item rarity does not necessarily mean a lower current contribution.

<!-- V4-C07-07 -->
Conversely, if both items are recognized, replacing rarity four with rarity three can reduce the slot's contribution from four to three. Recognition by more faiths may offer resilience to future changes, but does not guarantee a stronger result now. Decide what you want to preserve before choosing a candidate.

<!-- V4-C07-08 -->
These examples assume the items, slots, holder, and recognition are known. Unknown recognition is not rejection; an item you cannot observe is not automatically zero. Missing information can undermine the whole comparison.

<!-- V4-C07-09 -->
The total is only the beginning of the benefit calculation. You must still check what the type scales with it, which version of the value is read, and who meets the recipient conditions. A total of six does not add six to every relevant character attribute.

## Why an old total can persist after abandoning a site

<!-- V4-C08-01 -->
The Baghdad experiment exposed this distinction. We spent 1,000 Piety to remove the site from our faith's list, reducing the balance from 2,250 to 1,250. Other faiths remained associated with it, and the relic stayed in its slot.

<!-- V4-C08-02 -->
The County holder still followed our faith, but our faith had abandoned the site. Recalculating from current conditions therefore gave a total of zero and an inactive result. The cached state read at that moment still held a total of two and an active result.

<!-- V4-C08-03 -->
Those are two different questions: what do the conditions produce now, and what result did the game store earlier? Querying again while paused does not itself refresh every cache. An old display cannot substitute for checking current conditions.

<!-- V4-C08-04 -->
There is another layer. Some scripts that scale effects by rarity read the game's cached total. Character modifiers already aggregated for the panel also have their own update process. A fresh calculation of zero does not prove that every character's attributes have already been cleared.

<!-- V4-C08-06 -->
If you encounter this, do not keep investing just because an old icon remains active. Check the current holder and faith association, then verify the particular modifier you need. Avoid budgeting long-term gains from an old total after its underlying conditions have changed.

## Enshrining and replacing a relic

<!-- V4-C09-01 -->
Back at Nadjaf, it was already Eminent, but all three slots were empty. We then enshrined the Cloak of Muhammad. It moved from the emperor's ownership into the site, the qualifying rarity total rose from zero to four, and this step took no additional Piety.

<!-- V4-C09-02 -->
That confirms the enshrinement, but does not attribute every attribute change at that moment to the cloak. Elevation and enshrinement solve different problems. Empty slots do not rule out every fixed benefit; an active site does not prove that everyone received a bonus.

<!-- V4-C09-03 -->
Next, we directly swapped another rarity 4 relic for the cloak. The new item entered Nadjaf, and the old cloak went to the emperor performing the action, not to the County holder. In this case, those were two different characters.

<!-- V4-C09-04 -->
The qualifying rarity total stayed at four, and Piety did not change. The preview still listed opinion consequences, however. The cost is not only Piety: consider who may be affected, rather than assuming that no charge means no one loses.

<!-- V4-C09-05 -->
A direct swap handles the incoming and outgoing items within one enshrinement action. Removing an item manually and then enshrining another is a different route, with potentially different costs and conditions. The requirements for both items must still be satisfied.

<!-- V4-C09-06 -->
A normal removal gives the item to the current actor. In a direct swap, the outgoing item also goes to that actor. The rule does not look up the original donor and send it back. Once enshrined, a relic is not a secure personal deposit.

<!-- V4-C09-07 -->
A swap therefore has opportunity costs on both sides. You can find a new use for the returned item, but give up the incoming item's personal use. Even with the same rarity total and no Piety change, compare equipping, gifting, enshrining elsewhere, and recognition by different faiths.

<!-- V4-C09-08 -->
Check the slot as well. Automatic selection first looks for a compatible empty slot. If none exists, it considers compatible occupied slots, preferring the item with lower rarity and retaining the original order for ties. It does not compare recognition, political costs, or personal equipment value.

<!-- V4-C09-09 -->
If an existing item must be preserved, do not judge only the new item. Confirm the destination slot and exactly which relic will leave, then read the full preview. Automatic slot selection decides where an item fits, not which choice offers the best value.

## Who can manage it, including the AI?

<!-- V4-C10-01 -->
The territorial management check recognizes three roles: the County holder, that holder's top liege, and the holder of the site's Clerical Region. Being an intermediate liege alone does not authorize you to manage every holy site in the realm.

<!-- V4-C10-02 -->
Head of Faith is not a fourth, independent permission. A Head of Faith who also holds one of those three roles qualifies through that role. Their religious title alone does not authorize them to move every associated relic.

<!-- V4-C10-03 -->
Enshrinement also requires the site to belong to the actor's faith, the new item to come from the normal selection of personally owned items, recognition by the County holder's current faith, and a valid slot. The actor's faith and the holder's faith serve different checks here.

<!-- V4-C10-04 -->
A separate removal uses the same three territorial roles. It does not impose a universal extra requirement that the site belong to the actor's faith, or that their faith recognize the old item. Permission to take another faith's relic does not remove costs in Piety, Devotion, opinion, or possible war.

<!-- V4-C10-05 -->
Can AI characters move the relics? The base game has enshrinement and removal decisions for them. Relics are not moved only through player buttons. But a rule existing is different from observing someone actually use it in this campaign.

<!-- V4-C10-06 -->
The separate inventory-clearing decision has specific human-player protections: the top liege must be AI-controlled, and the relevant Heads of Faith must not be human players. The actor must also share their top liege's faith, be an adult who is available to act, and meet the zeal and domain requirements. This is not permission for any adult vassal to take anything.

<!-- V4-C10-07 -->
Its candidates include items its own faith does not recognize in that faith's holy sites, and items recognized by a hostile faith. The latter case also requires at least one County following that hostile faith. In a shared site, the actual clearing effect also protects the old item when this is the actor's holy site and their faith recognizes it.

<!-- V4-C10-08 -->
The human top-liege protection belongs specifically to that separate clearing decision. The enshrinement decision does not use the same set of human-player protections. It searches the actor's own items and faith's holy sites; if slot selection chooses an occupied slot, there is a route to replacing the old relic.

<!-- V4-C10-09 -->
The new item enters, and the old one goes to the AI actor. A previous human donation does not automatically block that route. A protection in one clearing decision does not make every donation permanently safe. Consider who can manage the site, and who may control it later.

<!-- V4-C10-10 -->
We have not filmed an AI character naturally performing these actions. We cannot claim that a particular vassal has already stolen a relic, or turn a configured tendency into a guaranteed yearly removal. This is an existing risk path to consider in your plans.

## Whose opinion changes, and why?

<!-- V4-C11-01 -->
Now consider opinion, starting with its direction: who thinks differently of whom? The modifiers discussed here change eligible characters' opinion of the actor. They do not change the actor's opinion of those characters, or apply one uniform score from one entire faith to another.

<!-- V4-C11-02 -->
A particular enshrinement reward branch selects direct vassals with the Zealot stance who recognize the new relic, lieges up the hierarchy who meet the zeal and recognition requirements, and the actor's eligible Head of Faith. The positive modifier to their opinion of the actor is 15.

<!-- V4-C11-03 -->
That does not mean donating a relic gives +15 with everyone of your faith. The relevant branch must apply, and each character must pass its relationship, stance, and recognition checks. The vassal selection is not uniformly restricted to the same faith. The Zealot stance, a zeal requirement, and a character trait are distinct checks.

<!-- V4-C11-04 -->
Removing an old item recognized by the actor's faith, without replacing it with an item of equal or greater rarity, enters the branch for that faith's loss. Eligible direct vassals of the same faith with Zealot or Minority stances, along with qualifying fellow vassals, lieges, and Heads of Faith, may lose opinion of the actor.

<!-- V4-C11-05 -->
The opinion penalty is 20, but each group has its own selection rules. The Head of Faith branch also checks that at least one County still follows the associated faith, that its Head exists, and that the Head recognizes the old item. Not every Head of an associated faith must object.

<!-- V4-C11-06 -->
An equal-or-better replacement can avoid that particular removal cost for the actor's faith. It does not ensure that everyone else is content. Another branch checks vassals, fellow vassals, and lieges of other faiths who recognize the old relic. That group does not use the same Zealot and Minority stance restrictions.

<!-- V4-C11-07 -->
Both positive and negative opinion modifiers last ten years and decay over time, rather than vanishing only on the final day. One character may fit several relationship roles or receive both modifiers. Do not multiply a modifier simply by counting the role names they match.

<!-- V4-C11-08 -->
Costs also depend on the branch. Suppose the actor's faith recognizes both the old rarity 4 item and the new rarity 3 item, and the relevant conditions continue to hold. Using these base values, enshrinement subtracts 250 Piety, followed by another 500 for removal: 750 in total. This is not a universal price for replacing rarity four with three.

<!-- V4-C11-09 -->
Under those base values, the Devotion loss is 500, not 750. A negative enshrinement difference subtracts only Piety; the removal branch subtracts both Piety and Devotion. But the game adjusts the amounts with resource modifiers first. Check the actual Piety balance, Devotion, and Level of Devotion separately, rather than presenting base values as this campaign's net loss.

<!-- V4-C11-10 -->
A warning about a war to reclaim a relic does not grant every ruler of another faith permission to declare one. Actual availability still depends on the two faiths, each side's recognition, whether the item can be transferred, and normal war requirements. AI rulers face additional zeal and title requirements.

<!-- V4-C11-11 -->
Before a swap, compare three accounts: your resources and Devotion, the uses lost and gained for the items, and the specific characters whose attitudes may change. If the preview warns of a risk, identify the people involved before deciding whether the Piety saved is worth it.

## What happens when a faith leaves?

<!-- V4-C12-01 -->
Demotion and abandonment do different things. Demotion frees an Eminent slot while keeping the place as your faith's holy site. Abandonment removes your faith's association. The normal decision also requires an ordinary site, sufficient remaining sites, and the relevant cooldown and eligibility checks.

<!-- V4-C12-02 -->
After we abandoned Baghdad, other faiths remained associated and the relic stayed in its slot. It was not packed up and returned to the emperor. Removing a shared site from one faith's list does not recover everything that faith's members once contributed.

<!-- V4-C12-03 -->
Removing your association may also leave the County holder following a faith that is no longer associated with the site. That changes the current relic calculation. The number of other faiths still recognizing the place does not guarantee that the old benefits continue unchanged.

<!-- V4-C12-04 -->
In another, independent branch, we created an ordinary Site of Wisdom in Samarra. Only our faith was associated, and the rarity 4 cloak occupied a slot. After the last faith abandoned it, we observed both its associations and relic inventory empty. The cloak was in the emperor's personal inventory, still unequipped. The site itself remained; it had not been erased from the map.

<!-- V4-C12-05 -->
The return rule here gives the relic to the County holder. The emperor received the cloak because he also held that County. Two roles happening to belong to one person does not prove that abandoning the last association always returns items to the actor.

<!-- V4-C12-06 -->
If a vassal held the County instead, this last-faith rule could give the relic to the vassal, not the emperor or the original donor. A different faith, or lack of recognition, is no reason to substitute a different recipient for the one specified by that rule.

<!-- V4-C12-07 -->
Remember the three destinations. Normal removal gives the item to the actor. A direct swap gives the outgoing item to the actor. When the last faith abandons the site, that rule gives the relic to the County holder. If another faith remains associated, the last-faith return procedure does not run.

<!-- V4-C12-08 -->
A returned cloak has not necessarily restored your equipment bonuses. Ownership, presence in your personal inventory, and being equipped are three different states. You still need to decide how to use it.

<!-- V4-C12-09 -->
If recovering a relic is your goal, first check your eligibility and cost for normal removal. Then ask whether abandoning the entire site is necessary. Abandonment also gives up association and future benefits, may cost more, and may deliver the item to someone else.

## Adopt an existing site or create a new one?

<!-- V4-C13-01 -->
Return to the original choice. Adopting an existing site can meet a need for a specific location. Creation lets you choose a suitable type and place. Both use capacity under the total holy site limit and have further requirements. The Piety shown on the button is not the whole comparison.

<!-- V4-C13-02 -->
Special buildings are one example. Some require the site to belong to the holder's faith and satisfy other religious conditions. Others allow alternatives. If another route already qualifies, an extra holy site association should not be treated as a mandatory unlock fee.

<!-- V4-C13-03 -->
Pilgrimage needs a separate calculation. A new association may add a destination worth considering, but the activity, recent visits, distance, travel costs, and safety still matter. The Hajj still goes to Mecca; creating a Site of Wisdom does not replace it.

<!-- V4-C13-04 -->
For your own Learning and construction, first examine existing Baghdad and the Counties you hold. For clergy support and Piety, compare Nadjaf's Eminent modifiers. If existing sites are underused, adding a new name may not be the best first expense.

<!-- V4-C13-05 -->
The Site of Wisdom branch began at 4,250 Piety, without first abandoning Nadjaf. Creation cost 2,000, leaving 2,250. Later, abandoning the last association cost another 1,000, leaving 1,250. This is one separate, continuous account.

<!-- V4-C13-06 -->
The new site is ordinary, the cloak is slotted, and the player holds the County. Eminent status is not included. If you also want the Site of Wisdom's Diplomacy and Lifestyle Experience bonuses, check Eminent capacity, cost, and eligibility separately. Do not count those gains as part of ordinary creation.

<!-- V4-C13-07 -->
Suppose the site has not yet been adopted, an old Eminent site must first be demoted, and the new one elevated. If each of the three current quotes is 1,000 and no free actions remain, budget 3,000. This is a conditional plan, not a 3,000-Piety bill assembled from separate experiments.

<!-- V4-C13-08 -->
The three free actions granted when a faith is created are a finite pool shared by that faith. Each ruler does not get a fresh set of three, and succession does not replenish them. Using one now also gives up the chance to save it for a later step.

<!-- V4-C13-09 -->
Check each cooldown separately. Do not assume adoption, elevation, demotion, abandonment, and creation share one lock just because their durations look similar. Before taking the first step, list the capacity, resources, authority, and cooldown required for the next.

<!-- V4-C13-10 -->
Compare the whole set of options: improve an existing site, adopt another, create a suitable type, or keep your resources. Weigh the new benefits against the old Eminent effects lost, the relic's personal uses, opinion costs, and how long you can retain control of the location.

<!-- V4-C13-11 -->
A longer list and a higher grade do not automatically make a better holy site plan. Identify the intended recipient and goal, then check association, the holder's faith, recognition in each slot, and who receives outgoing items. The button is worth pressing when the investment serves the purpose you actually need.
